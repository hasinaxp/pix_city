Pedestrians
Collision avoidance — naturally sidestep people instead of clipping through them.
Crowd movement — pedestrians form natural flows without bunching together.
Physical impacts — different reactions to light pushes, hard hits, and vehicle impacts.
Stumbling — lose balance when pushed or hit lightly, then recover.
Falling — ragdoll on strong impacts and intelligently get back up.
Path obstruction — walk around parked cars, debris, blocked sidewalks, etc.
Road crossing — use crossings, wait for traffic, and sometimes cross dangerously.
Group behavior — friends walk together, stop together, and separate naturally.
Daily routines — commute, shop, eat, socialize, return home.
Idle behavior — phones, conversations, smoking, sitting, looking around.
Reactions
Player awareness — look at unusual player behavior and maintain personal space.
Threat detection — react differently to weapons, aiming, violence, and nearby danger.
Gunshots — investigate, freeze, hide, run, or call police depending on personality.
Explosions — immediate panic followed by fleeing and avoiding the area.
Car crashes — stop, look, approach, flee, or call emergency services.
Fights — gather around, intervene, record, flee, or call police.
Dead bodies — avoid the area, investigate briefly, or report them.
Police presence — slow down, move aside, watch, flee, or cooperate.
Fire — flee buildings/streets and warn nearby pedestrians.
Crowd panic — NPCs seeing others panic can become frightened themselves.
Personality
Bravery — determines whether NPCs investigate or flee.
Aggression — determines willingness to confront other NPCs.
Curiosity — determines how strongly unusual events attract them.
Lawfulness — affects whether they report crimes and cooperate with police.
Friendliness — affects interactions with strangers.
Fear response — different NPCs have different panic thresholds.
Memory — NPCs remember major encounters with the player.
Relationships — friends/family react when someone they know is attacked.
Vehicles
Driver awareness — detect pedestrians, vehicles, obstacles, and unusual events.
Emergency braking — vehicles react physically rather than simply stopping.
Swerving — drivers avoid sudden obstacles and collisions.
Honking — frustration and warnings based on traffic situations.
Lane changes — naturally change lanes around slow vehicles and obstacles.
Traffic incidents — crashes and stalled vehicles create realistic congestion.
Vehicle damage — tires, doors, windows, bumpers, lights, and body damage affect behavior.
Loss of control — collisions can cause spinning, sliding, or rollovers.
Emergency vehicles — traffic moves aside and pedestrians react to sirens.
Environment
Breakable objects — signs, fences, lamps, windows, bins, barriers.
Movable objects — chairs, tables, trash cans, cones and street furniture react to impacts.
Physical doors — doors open, close, slam, and react to collisions.
Debris — crashes generate objects that remain temporarily in the environment.
Glass physics — windows crack, break, and produce physical shards.
Weather interaction — rain affects traction, puddles, visibility, and pedestrian behavior.
Wind interaction — papers, signs, umbrellas, trash and lightweight objects react.
Event Chains
Crash → traffic jam — nearby drivers brake, change lanes and reroute.
Crash → crowd — pedestrians stop and investigate.
Gunshot → panic — nearby NPCs flee and spread panic to others.
Fight → police call — witnesses report the incident.
Fire → evacuation — pedestrians leave buildings and emergency vehicles arrive.
Police chase → traffic reaction — cars yield while pedestrians move away.
Major incident → persistent aftermath — debris, damaged vehicles, crowds and emergency services remain for a while.
